💥 2. Yes — the ship can eventually be destroyed by gravity
But only under extreme conditions, and only after a chain of realistic failures.
Here are the exact scenarios where gravity can kill the ship:
🪐 Example 1 — Descending into Jupiter’s atmosphere
Gravity at Jupiter cloud tops: 24.79 m/s² Deep layers: >30 m/s²
Your ship reacts like this:
12–25 m/s² (high‑g zone)
    • Hull integrity slowly decreases
    • Internal oxygen drains faster
    • Computer takes stress damage
    • Hover mode becomes unstable
    • Pulse engine becomes harder to control
>25 m/s² (extreme‑g zone)
    • Hull integrity drops rapidly
    • Internal pressure collapses
    • Electronics glitch
    • Overheating accelerates
    • Eventually the ship breaks apart
This is a slow, dramatic failure, not instant death.
🌑 Example 2 — Falling into a deep gravity well (e.g., close to a neutron star mod)
If you place the ship near a body with:
    • >30 m/s² gravity

    • Overheat the hull
    • Overload the computer
    • Collapse internal pressure
    • Drain oxygen
    • Cause structural failure
Again — not instant, but inevitable.
🌋 Example 3 — Hovering too long on a high‑gravity world
Let’s say you land on a hypothetical super‑Earth with:
    • 15–20 m/s² gravity
Hover mode must fight gravity constantly.
What happens:
    • Main engine runs near max throttle
    • Fuel drains rapidly
    • Hull integrity slowly degrades from stress
    • Computer takes strain damage
    • If fuel runs out → ship falls → impact damage → destruction
Gravity didn’t “kill” the ship directly — it forced the ship into a failure cascade.
🪨 Example 4 — Crash landing due to gravity overpowering thrust
If gravity > thrust capability:
    • Hover mode can’t maintain altitude
    • Ship descends
    • RCS struggles
    • Pulse engine can’t be used in atmosphere
    • Impact destroys hull integrity
This is realistic and matches your design goals.
